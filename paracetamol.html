Walk through the paracetamol overdose pathway

A step-by-step interpretation of the paracetamol overdose pathway. Enter the patient's weight, type of ingestion, and timing — the tool returns recommended actions, weight-banded SNAP acetylcysteine dosing, and post-infusion review criteria.

Investigations

Bloods to take

Same bundle at presentation and at end of Bag 2. Request via the PICS/ICE "overdose" bundle.

On presentation — baseline

Record the patient's weight clearly in the notes — it guides further assessment and antidote dosing.

  • Paracetamol concentration — at ≥ 4 hours post ingestion
  • FBC
  • INR
  • U&Es
  • LFTs — including ALT
  • Venous blood gas — pH, lactate, bicarbonate, glucose

End of Bag 2 — post-infusion review

Take at — or just before — the end of Bag 2. Use the result to decide whether to stop or continue acetylcysteine.

  • Paracetamol concentration — target: undetectable
  • INR — target: < 1.3
  • U&Es
  • LFTs / ALT — target: within normal reference range
  • Venous blood gas
Discharge after Bag 2 requires all four criteria: INR < 1.3, ALT within the normal range, paracetamol undetectable, and no symptoms of liver damage. Otherwise repeat Bag 2 and recheck at the end. Open for the interactive checklist.

Paracetamol overdose treatment graph

For acute single overdose with a known ingestion time. Source: BNF — Poisoning, emergency treatment.

Patients whose plasma-paracetamol concentrations are on or above the treatment line should be treated with acetylcysteine by intravenous infusion.

The prognostic accuracy after 15 hours is uncertain, but a plasma-paracetamol concentration on or above the treatment line should be regarded as carrying a serious risk of liver damage.

Post-infusion review (end of Bag 2)

Take bloods at — or just before — the end of Bag 2: U&Es, LFTs, INR, VBG, paracetamol concentration. Then check the four discontinuation criteria below.

Discontinuation criteria — tick those that are met

If a 3rd bag is given — repeat criteria at end of Bag 3

Discharge is safe if all are met:

  • INR ≤ 1.3 AND
  • ALT < 2× upper limit of normal AND
  • ALT not more than double the admission measurement

Otherwise, continue acetylcysteine at the same dose and rate.

Liaison Psychiatry referral is required prior to discharge for all patients who have taken a deliberate overdose.
Reference

Adverse reactions to acetylcysteine

Anaphylactoid (not anaphylactic) reactions — usually within the first 30 minutes of Bag 1. Generally not life-threatening.

Common features

Nausea, vomiting, flushing, urticarial rash, tachycardia, wheeze. More likely in women, asthmatics, patients with family history of allergy, or when paracetamol concentration is low or absent.

Management
  • Pause the infusion (this may be all that is required).
  • Chlorphenamine 10 mg IV (H₁ antihistamine).
  • Salbutamol 2.5 mg nebulised if bronchospasm is significant.
  • Ondansetron 4 mg IV for nausea / vomiting.
  • Restart treatment once the reaction has settled — consider reducing the rate.

Previous reaction to acetylcysteine

Not a contraindication for a further course. Consider prophylactic chlorphenamine 10 mg IV and ondansetron 4 mg IV, with PRN nebulised salbutamol 2.5 mg in case of bronchospasm.

Markers of poor prognosis — seek liver registrar advice
  • INR > 3.0
  • Plasma creatinine > 200 micromol/L
  • Blood pH < 7.3
  • Encephalopathy (confusion, drowsiness, disorientation, asterixis)
Patients with incipient or established hepatic failure may be transplant candidates — discuss with the liver team at QEHB.
